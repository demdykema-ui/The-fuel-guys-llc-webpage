Understanding Off-Road Diesel

What Is Red Dye Diesel?

Red dye diesel — also called dyed diesel, off-road diesel, or non-taxed diesel — is regular #2 diesel fuel with a red dye added. The dye marks it as tax-exempt, meaning it is not subject to federal and state highway fuel taxes.

It is chemically identical to clear (on-road) diesel. Same performance. Same quality. Same cetane rating. The only difference is the tax status and the red dye itself.

Why is it red? The IRS requires the red dye so that enforcement agents can test whether a vehicle on public roads is running tax-exempt fuel. Using red dye diesel in a highway vehicle is illegal and carries severe penalties.

Know the Rules

Who Can Legally Use Red Dye Diesel?

If your equipment never drives on public highways, you likely qualify

Approved Uses

  • Construction equipment — excavators, loaders, dozers, skid steers, cranes
  • Generators — backup power, job site generators, event generators
  • Agricultural equipment — tractors, combines, irrigation pumps
  • Mining and forestry equipment
  • Stationary engines and pumps
  • Any off-road equipment that does not drive on public highways

Prohibited Uses

  • Vehicles registered for highway use (trucks, pickups, vans driving on public roads)
  • Any engine powering a vehicle on public highways or streets

Penalties for illegal use: The IRS imposes a minimum penalty of $10 per gallon of dyed fuel found in a highway vehicle, plus back taxes owed, plus potential criminal charges for repeat offenders.

Consult your tax advisor for your specific situation. Tax rules vary by use case and jurisdiction.
