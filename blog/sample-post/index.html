How Often Should a Job Site Generator Be Refueled in Denver?

Quick answer

Most job-site generators in Denver need refueling every 1 to 3 days. A generator burning 3 to 6 gallons per hour under load will empty a 100-gallon tank in about a day of continuous use. Scheduled mobile diesel delivery keeps tanks full without pulling crews off the job.

By Erick Dykema Published Jul 10, 2026 Updated Jul 10, 2026 5 min read Answers

Generators are the quiet backbone of most Denver job sites — they run the trailers, the chargers, the pumps, and in winter the heaters that keep concrete curing. The one thing they cannot do is fuel themselves. This article covers how fast common generator sizes burn diesel, what running dry actually costs, and how scheduled mobile refueling keeps a site powered without anyone hauling cans.

How much diesel does a construction generator burn?

Burn rate depends on the generator's size and how hard it is working. The figures below are typical for diesel generators running at roughly three-quarters load, which is common on an active site.

Generator size Typical burn rate (under load) Runtime on 100 gallons
20 kW~1.6 gal/hour~60 hours
60 kW~2.9 gal/hour~34 hours
100 kW~4.1 gal/hour~24 hours
150 kW~6.1 gal/hour~16 hours

The practical takeaway: a mid-size generator running through a work day plus overnight heaters can empty its tank in one to two days. Sites running around the clock — dewatering pumps, winter heat, event power — are usually on a daily fill.

What happens if a generator runs dry?

Running out of diesel is more than an inconvenience. When a diesel engine sucks air, the fuel system usually needs to be bled and primed before it will restart, and on many units that means a service call. In the meantime:

  • Job trailers, chargers, and site security systems lose power.
  • Concrete heaters and dewatering pumps stop when they are needed most.
  • Crews stand around while someone drives to a fuel station with cans.

How does scheduled generator refueling work?

Scheduled mobile refueling replaces the fuel-can run entirely. A delivery truck comes to the site on a set cadence — daily, every other day, or weekly depending on burn rate — and tops off the generator directly. Fuel is metered at the nozzle, so the invoice reflects the exact gallons pumped. Most sites pair generator fills with equipment and tank fills in the same stop, which keeps the per-visit cost down.

The right cadence comes straight from the table above: estimate your generator's daily burn, divide your tank size by it, and schedule deliveries comfortably inside that window.

Frequently asked questions

Can you refuel a generator while it is running?

In most cases the generator is shut down briefly during refueling, which takes only a few minutes. Some larger units have a separate day tank or fill port that allows fueling without interrupting power. Our drivers follow the procedure the equipment manufacturer and the site supervisor specify.

Do you deliver dyed diesel for generators in Denver?

Yes. Stationary generators are off-road equipment, so they can legally run on red-dyed diesel, which is not charged federal and state road taxes. That typically makes dyed diesel the lower-cost choice for generator fueling on Denver job sites and at commercial facilities.

How much notice do you need for a scheduled generator refill?

For recurring refills on a set route, no notice is needed once the schedule is in place. For a first-time or one-off generator fill in the Denver metro, same-day and next-day windows are usually available. Emergency fills are handled around the clock.

What size tanks can a mobile fuel truck fill?

Mobile delivery covers everything from small portable generator tanks under 50 gallons up to stationary tanks of several thousand gallons. The fuel is metered at the point of delivery, so you are billed for the exact gallons pumped regardless of tank size.
